Decarbonisation is a form of structural change that governments choose to make. Unlike job losses from trade or automation, which are often described as forces of nature, the employment costs of climate policy have clear political authors. My research examines what follows from that: how legislators represent workers in high-emitting industries, how citizens respond when jobs are lost to policy rather than markets, and how protest and media coverage shape party positions on fossil fuels.
